A gateway game turns a non-player into a player. It can be taught in under ten minutes. It finishes in under an hour. And it forgives early mistakes, so a bad first move does not ruin the night.

The best beginner games are Ticket to Ride, Codenames, Azul, Carcassonne and Sushi Go!. This guide explains why each one works, and which one to buy first.

One warning before you start. Do not buy a game that takes three hours and has a 40-page rulebook. That game can wait. Tonight is for learning, laughing, and finishing what you started.

## 1. Ticket to Ride: the best first game for most people

Ticket to Ride is a railway game for 2–5 players, 45–60 minutes, ages 8 and up. You collect coloured train cards and spend them to claim routes across a map of the USA, trying to connect the cities on your secret destination tickets.

It is the definitive gateway game. The goal is visible, the rules fit on a few pages, and a bad early move costs you points. It does not ruin your evening.

If you only buy one game from this list, buy this one.

## 2. Carcassonne: build a landscape together

Carcassonne is a tile-laying game for 2–5 players, 35–45 minutes, ages 7 and up. Each turn you draw a tile and place it to grow a shared landscape of cities, roads and fields, then decide whether to put one of your pieces on it.

Carcassonne is quieter than Ticket to Ride. There is no hand of cards to manage, no route to rush. You just place a tile, make a small decision, and watch the map grow.

It is the best pick if your group likes calm games, and it is one of the easiest games on this list to teach. The rules are genuinely simple, and the depth comes from the choices, not from the rulebook.

## 3. Codenames: the party game

Codenames is a word game for 4 or more players that takes 15–20 minutes. Two teams race to find their secret words from a grid, guided by one-word clues.

It is the fastest game here to teach, and the best for a big group, but you need at least four people, and it is better with six or more. Do not buy it for a couple; the two-player version is Codenames: Duet.

## 4. Azul: the beautiful one

Azul is a tile game for 2–4 players, 30–45 minutes, ages 8 and up. You draft coloured tiles and arrange them into patterns. It is tactile.

The tiles are chunky and satisfying to handle, and it wins people over before you have even opened the rulebook.

It is a gentle strategy game: you build your own pattern, and the only way to hurt another player is to take a tile they wanted.

## 5. Splendor: quiet, clever, thirty minutes

Splendor is a card game for 2–4 players that takes about 30 minutes. You collect gems, buy development cards, and build an engine that lets you buy better cards. It is quiet, it is elegant, and it is over in half an hour.

Splendor is the best pick for two adults who want a thoughtful game without a lot of noise or table talk. It is also one of the easiest games here to learn: the rules take about five minutes to explain, and the strategy reveals itself over repeat plays.

## 6. Sushi Go! (fast, silly, fifteen minutes)

Sushi Go! is a card-drafting game for 2–5 players, 15–20 minutes. You pick a card, pass the hand on, and try to build the best sushi meal. The art is cute, the rounds are quick, and the whole game fits in a small box you can carry in a bag.

Buy Sushi Go! if you want something light, cheap-feeling and fast. It is a good first game for children as well as adults, and it is a good “one more game” filler after a longer game.

## 7. Kingdomino: twenty minutes, works for everyone

Kingdomino is a domino-style game for 2–4 players, 15–25 minutes, ages 8 and up. You draft domino tiles and build a little kingdom. It plays in about twenty minutes, it works beautifully with children and adults at the same table, and it won the Spiel des Jahres, the German game-of-the-year award.

Kingdomino is the shortest real strategy game on this list. If someone says they only have twenty minutes, this is the answer.

## 8. Cascadia: the gentlest strategy game

Cascadia is a tile-laying game for 1–4 players, 30–45 minutes, ages 10 and up. You build a landscape of habitats and place animals in it, matching terrain to score points. There is no direct conflict, no taking things from opponents, and it has a solo mode so you can practise alone.

Cascadia is the gentlest strategy game here. It is the pick for a group that wants to think, but does not want to fight. It is also the newest game on this list.

It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2022, and it has quietly become a modern classic.

## 9. Patchwork: the two-player specialist

Patchwork is a game for exactly two players, 15–30 minutes, ages 8 and up. You build a quilt from oddly shaped patches, buying pieces with buttons. Its BoardGameGeek rating is 7.6 out of 10, and its complexity is 1.6 out of 5.

Most gateway games are best with three or more players. Patchwork fills the gap: it is built exclusively for two. For couples and roommates, it is the easiest recommendation on this list.

## 10. Just One: everyone wins or loses together

Just One is a cooperative party game for 3–7 players, about 20 minutes. Everyone writes a one-word clue to help the guesser find a secret word, but if two people write the same clue, it is cancelled.

Just One is the best pick for a mixed group where some people are competitive and some are not. There is no winner-takes-all, the rules take two minutes to explain, and the game is genuinely funny when clues get cancelled.

## How to choose your first game

- **One game for a family?** Ticket to Ride.
- **One game for a couple?** Patchwork.
- **One game for a party?** Codenames.
- **One game for a quiet evening?** Azul or Cascadia.
- **Only twenty minutes?** Kingdomino or Sushi Go!.
- **Want to play alone sometimes?** Cascadia. It has a solo mode.

## What beginners get wrong

- **Buying five games at once.** Buy one, learn it, play it five times. Then buy a second.
- **Starting with a heavy game.** If the rulebook takes longer to read than the game takes to play, you started in the wrong place.
- **Skipping the player count.** A game for 4 or more players is a bad buy if you will mostly play with two.
- **Judging a game by one play.** The first game is confusing. The second game is the real one.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the easiest board game to learn?**
Kingdomino and Ticket to Ride are the two we would pick. Both can be taught in a few minutes, and both are fun on the first play.

**What is the best board game for a complete beginner?**
Ticket to Ride. It is the most reliable first modern board game: 2–5 players, 45–60 minutes, ages 8 and up, and it works for families, couples and mixed groups.
