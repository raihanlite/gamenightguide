Playing a board game alone sounds strange until you try it. Then it makes sense: a quiet puzzle, no scheduling, no waiting for other people, and nobody watching you lose. Solo board gaming has seen a surge in popularity over the past few years, and the community is bigger than most people realise — the BoardGameGeek 2026 People’s Choice Top Solo Games vote drew a record 2,139 voters, 2,168 unique games, and 32,584 votes.

But solo games have a trap. The games that solo players talk about most — Spirit Island, Terraforming Mars, Mage Knight, Gloomhaven — are big, expensive, and complicated. They are excellent games. They are terrible first solo games. This guide starts with the gentle ones, and tells you honestly when the big ones are worth it.

## What “solo” actually means

Solo board games come in three kinds, and the difference matters more than the game’s name.

- **Designed for one player.** Games like Friday were made to be played alone. The rules, the puzzle and the difficulty are all built for one person.
- **A solo mode in a multiplayer game.** Cascadia is a game for 1–4 players. Its solo mode is a real mode, not an afterthought: you play the same puzzle, alone, against a target score.
- **A multiplayer game you can play alone.** Some games can be played solo by controlling two sides, but that is not really solo play — it is two-player play with one person doing all the work. Avoid this for your first solo game.

For a beginner, the first two kinds are the right ones. The third kind is a chore.

## 1. Cascadia — the best first solo game

Cascadia is a tile-laying game for 1–4 players, 30–45 minutes, ages 10 and up. You build a landscape of habitats and place animals in it, matching terrain to score points. There is no direct conflict, and the solo mode is a proper solo mode: you build your landscape and try to beat a target score.

Cascadia is the best first solo game for three reasons. It is calm, so a solo session feels relaxing rather than stressful. It is short, so a game fits into a lunch break. And it is a game you can also play with other people, so it is not a game that only works alone. If you buy one solo game, buy this one — and if you already own it for family nights, you already own a solo game.

## 2. Friday — small, cheap, and honest

Friday is a small solo card game, made for exactly one player. You are Robinson Crusoe, trying to survive on an island, and the game is a deck of cards that gets harder as you play. A game is quick — the whole game fits in a box the size of a paperback.

Friday is the cheapest way to find out whether you like solo games. It is quick, it is hard — you will lose more often than you win, and that is the point — and it teaches you the most important solo skill: playing against the game, not against a person. If you play Friday five times and want more, you are a solo player. If you play it once and never touch it again, you have learned that cheaply.

## 3. A Gentle Rain — fifteen minutes of quiet

A Gentle Rain is a solo tile game for one player, about 15 minutes, rated 7.6 out of 10 on BoardGameGeek with a complexity of 1.1 out of 5 — about as simple as a board game gets. You place tiles to make a lake, and the game is over in a quarter of an hour.

This is the game for a night when you are tired and want something gentle. It is not deep, it is not dramatic, and it does not pretend to be. It is a puzzle you can finish before bed. It is also a good test: if fifteen quiet minutes with a board game sounds appealing, solo play is for you. If it sounds boring, it probably is not.

## The big three — what they cost, and when to buy them

These are the games solo players talk about most — and the games beginners should not start with.

### Spirit Island — $57.99

Spirit Island is a cooperative strategy game that can be played solo. It is rated 8.4 out of 10 on BoardGameGeek, it takes 90–120 minutes, and it costs $57.99 — price verified 22 Nov 2026. It earned the number one spot in the BoardGameGeek People’s Choice solo poll.

Spirit Island is deep, replayable, and genuinely excellent. It is also long, complicated, and expensive for a first solo game. Buy it after you have played Cascadia or Friday for a few weeks and you want something bigger — not before.

### Terraforming Mars — $79.99

Terraforming Mars is an engine-building game that can be played solo. It is rated 8.4 out of 10 on BoardGameGeek, it takes about 120 minutes, and it costs $79.99 — price verified 22 Nov 2026.

Two hours, eighty dollars, and a lot of rules. Terraforming Mars is a wonderful game for someone who already knows they like long strategy games. It is a bad first solo game, and a bad gift for someone who has never played one.

### Mage Knight — $79.99

Mage Knight is a big adventure game that can be played solo. It is rated 8.1 out of 10 on BoardGameGeek, it takes 60–240 minutes — a full afternoon — and it costs $79.99, price verified 22 Nov 2026.

Mage Knight is famous among solo players, and famously hard to learn. The rulebook is long, the first game will take hours, and you will get rules wrong. Buy it only when you are sure solo gaming is your hobby, not when you are testing whether it might be.

## How to start solo play — a checklist

- [ ] **Start with a game that has a real solo mode.** Cascadia is the safest pick. Friday is the cheapest.
- [ ] **Keep the first session under 45 minutes.** A long first solo game that drags will put you off the hobby.
- [ ] **Play the same game three times.** The first game is learning the rules. The second is learning the strategy. The third is the real game.
- [ ] **Read the solo rules before you buy.** A game that says “1–4 players” does not always have a good solo mode. Check that the solo mode is a real mode, not a two-player game played alone.

## What solo play is good for — and what it is not

Solo play is good for a quiet evening, for learning a game before you teach it to friends, and for people whose friends are busy. It is not a replacement for game night. The best solo games are puzzles, and puzzles are best in small doses.

## Frequently asked questions

Cascadia. It is 1–4 players, 30–45 minutes, ages 10 and up, it has a real solo mode, and it is calm enough to enjoy alone.

**What is the cheapest way to try solo board games?**
Friday. It is a small solo card game, a game takes 15–30 minutes, and it is one of the cheapest games in the hobby. Its price was not verified for this guide on 22 Nov 2026 — check the store before you buy.

**Is Spirit Island a good first solo game?**
No. It is rated 8.4 out of 10, but it takes 90–120 minutes and costs $57.99. Play a shorter solo game first.

**Can I play a multiplayer game solo?**
Some games have a proper solo mode, and some do not. Cascadia does. Controlling two sides yourself is not really solo play.
