Ticket to Ride and Catan are the two most recommended first board games in the world. Ask any board game fan what to buy first, and one of these two names will be in the answer.

They are both gateway games. Both are easy to learn. Both have been introducing people to modern board games for more than twenty years.

They are not the same game, and they are not for the same people. This guide compares them on the facts, and tells you which one to buy for your table.

## What each game actually is

### Ticket to Ride

Ticket to Ride is a railway game. You collect coloured train cards and spend them to claim routes across a map of the USA, trying to connect the cities on your secret destination tickets. The player with the most points at the end wins.

The rules are simple: on your turn, you draw cards, claim a route, or draw new destination tickets. That is almost the whole game. The depth comes from the choices, which route to claim before someone else takes it, and whether to risk a long destination ticket for big points.

### Catan

Catan is a game about settling an island. You collect resources, wood, brick, sheep, wheat and ore, by rolling dice, trade them with other players, and spend them to build settlements, cities and roads. The first player to ten points wins.

The rules are simple too, but the game is social in a way Ticket to Ride is not. Trading is the heart of Catan. You will spend half the game talking to the other players: offering wood for brick, asking who needs sheep, and deciding whether to help someone or block them.

If your group likes to talk, Catan is the better game. If your group likes quiet concentration, it is not.

## The five differences that matter

### 1. Player count

Ticket to Ride plays 2–5 players. Catan plays 3–4 players. This is the first thing to check, because it decides the question for a lot of people.

If you will mostly play with two people, neither game is ideal. Read our [couples guide](/guides/best-board-games-for-couples/) instead. But Ticket to Ride at two is a workable game, while Catan at two is not really Catan at all: the trading, which is the whole point, has nobody to trade with.

If you will play with five, only Ticket to Ride works. Catan stops at four.

### 2. Time

Ticket to Ride takes 45–60 minutes. Catan takes 60–90 minutes. That half hour matters for a first game.

A 45-minute game that ends while everyone is still having fun is a success. A 90-minute game that drags past bedtime is a failure, however good the game is.

For a family with children, or for a first game night, Ticket to Ride’s shorter time is a real advantage.

### 3. Talking vs. quiet

Catan is a talking game. You trade on almost every turn, and the game is as much about reading the table as it is about the board. Some people love this.

Some people hate it, especially the moment when three players gang up on the leader, or when nobody will trade with you and you sit with a hand full of sheep.

Ticket to Ride is a quiet game. You are mostly thinking about your own routes, and the interaction is indirect: someone takes the route you wanted. There is no negotiation, no pleading, no table politics.

For a mixed group where some people are shy, or for a family with children who might take trading personally, Ticket to Ride is the safer choice.

### 4. Luck

Both games have luck. In Ticket to Ride, the luck is in the cards you draw. In Catan, the luck is in the dice: if the numbers you need do not come up, you get no resources, and there is nothing you can do about it.

Catan’s dice can feel more unfair, because a bad run of rolls can leave you with nothing for several turns while other players build. Ticket to Ride’s luck is gentler. You can always draw cards, and a bad draw just slows you down.

Neither game is a pure luck game. But if your group dislikes feeling unlucky, Ticket to Ride is the kinder of the two.

### 5. Age

Ticket to Ride is rated for ages 8 and up. Catan is rated for ages 10 and up. That two-year gap is real: Catan’s trading and resource management ask more of a younger player, and a child who cannot trade well will fall behind and stop enjoying the game.

For a family with an 8- or 9-year-old, Ticket to Ride is the right call.

## Which one should you buy?

- **Buy Ticket to Ride if:** you want the safest first game; you play with 2 or 5 people sometimes; your group is quiet, or includes children aged 8–9; you want a game under an hour.
- **Buy Catan if:** your group loves to talk and negotiate; you always play with 3–4 people; you want a game where the conversation is half the fun; your players are 10 or older.
- **Buy both, eventually:** they are different enough that owning both is not a waste. But buy one first, play it five times, and then buy the other. A shelf of unplayed games helps nobody.

## What about the other versions?

Both games have many versions. Ticket to Ride has Europe, and many city maps. Catan has a 6th edition, which is the current one, and expansions like Cities &amp; Knights.

For a first buy, ignore all of them. Buy the base game, Ticket to Ride (the USA map) or Catan (6th edition), and nothing else. Expansions add complexity and cost, and you do not need them to enjoy either game.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which is easier to learn, Ticket to Ride or Catan?**
Ticket to Ride, slightly. Both can be taught in about ten minutes, but Ticket to Ride’s rules are shorter and its goal is more visible. Catan’s trading takes a game or two to understand.

**Can you play Catan with 2 players?**
Catan is designed for 3–4 players. It is not a good two-player game. The trading, which is the heart of the game, does not work with two.

If you mostly play with two, buy a game made for two, like Patchwork or Jaipur.

**Is Ticket to Ride good with 2 players?**
It works, but it is a different game than with four or five. The board is emptier and blocking matters less. It is still fun, and it is better at two than Catan is, but a dedicated two-player game will be better still.

**Which game is better for kids?**
Ticket to Ride, for children aged 8–9. Catan is rated 10 and up, and its trading is hard for younger children. For older children, either works.

**Which one should I buy first?**
For most people, Ticket to Ride. It plays more player counts, it is shorter, it is quieter, and it is the safer first game. Buy Catan second, when you know your group likes to talk.
